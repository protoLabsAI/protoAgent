- **A watch can be a standing monitor, not just a one-shot tripwire (ADR 0067).** Until now a
  watch fired once and went terminal, so "every time X happens, do Y" had to be hand-composed
  from a watch plus an `on_met` hook that re-created it. Two orthogonal knobs now cover it:
  `trigger` picks what fires the reaction (`met`, the default, or `change`), and `repeat` picks
  whether firing ends the watch.

  - `met` + `repeat` fires on each **rising edge** of the predicate. Edge, not level,
    deliberately: a condition that latches — `credits >= 1,000,000`, true forever once crossed
    — would otherwise re-fire every tick for the life of the watch.
  - `trigger: change` is a monitor rather than a tripwire: it fires whenever the verifier's
    **evidence moves**, whatever the predicate says, and its first check only establishes the
    baseline. It calls the new `on_changed` hook and publishes `watch.value_changed` — not
    `on_met`/`watch.met`, since a plugin subscribed to those is being told the condition is
    satisfied, which a value merely moving doesn't mean.

  Available on every surface: `repeat`/`on_change` on the `create_watch` and `update_watch`
  tools, `trigger`/`repeat` on `sdk.create_watch` and `POST`/`PATCH /api/watches`, and a
  three-way **Fire** selector in the console creator. A repeating watch ends only at its
  deadline or an explicit clear, so the form nudges toward setting an expiry.

  Both dispositions are **instrumented for flapping** from day one, since a watch that fires
  back-to-back can enqueue an agent turn per tick. Each watch carries `check_count` /
  `fire_count` / `consecutive_fires` (visible in `GET /api/watches`, and as `fired N×` on the
  console row); crossing a consecutive-fire threshold logs a **deduped** warning naming the
  watch, trigger, interval and rate; and `protoagent_watch_fires_total{trigger}` +
  `protoagent_watch_flapping_total{trigger}` are on `/metrics`. Labelled by trigger only —
  operator-defined watch ids would be an unbounded-cardinality label.
