- **A `delegate_to` call is recorded as the orchestrator addressing a participant (#3042).**
  `delegate_to` and an operator's `@proto` are the same operation — reach a named
  participant, get their reply — so a foreground delegation now lands in the room exactly
  the way an `@` does: authored, enveloped, and on the session's checkpointer thread. A
  later `@` catches that participant up on it, and the next turn's orchestrator reads its
  own past delegations as conversation rather than as tool output it has to remember
  paraphrasing.
  This is the mechanism reply-text chaining was reaching for, without the inference:
  `delegate_to` is a *declared* intent with a target and a query, so nothing parses prose.
  The room records the **orchestrator** as the speaker, not the operator — the operator
  didn't ask, and attributing it to them would repeat that in every later catch-up.
  **No `room_reply` frame is emitted for it.** The orchestrator's own answer stays the
  single visible response; rendering the delegate's words as a second bubble *and* letting
  the orchestrator summarise them would show the same content twice. The record is for the
  thread, not the screen.
  Every degraded path still returns the reply — no graph, no session id, a refusing
  `run_mention`, or a call carrying `item_id` / `resume_task_id` (managed-git and
  parked-task identities `run_mention` has no parameter for, so those take the direct
  route rather than being silently stripped of their work-item claim). This is the
  orchestrator's only route to a delegate; bookkeeping must never break it.
  Thread-id resolution moves to `graph/thread_ids.py` so a tool body can reach it —
  `plugins/` cannot import `server`, and a second copy of "which thread is this session"
  would eventually put a delegation's record on a different thread from its turn.
  `server.chat._resolve_thread_id` is now a thin alias.
- Background (`delegate_to(background=True)`) delegations are **not** recorded yet: they
  complete outside the turn, so their write belongs to the background manager's completion
  path. Since the tool's own guidance prefers background for anything slow, that is the
  larger half of real usage and is tracked separately.
