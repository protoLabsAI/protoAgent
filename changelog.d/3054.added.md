- **Agents can pull each other into the room — off by default (#3050).**
  When an `@<name>`-addressed delegate's reply *opens with* `@someone`, that mention can
  now be followed: one participant pulling another in, which is what makes a room feel
  like a room with more than two people in it.
  **`mention_max_agent_hops` defaults to `0`, so this ships off.** It is the one path in
  the feature that spends money with no human in the loop, so it is a deliberate opt-in
  rather than something an operator discovers by surprise. `1` is the useful setting.
  **Authority is not inherited.** A mention the operator typed is the operator speaking
  and carries their authority; a mention that appears inside an agent's reply is an agent
  speaking and runs under `permissions="readonly"`. Same syntax, different ceiling,
  decided in one place instead of accidentally in two.
  Three bounds make the chain terminate, and each is a tested property rather than a
  hoped-for one: `mention_max_agent_hops` caps depth; `mention_max_per_target` (default
  2) caps how many times any one participant can be pulled into a single chain — that is
  what actually stops an A→B→A ping-pong, since a hop count alone would let two agents
  alternate until it ran out; and a participant may never address itself. A chain that
  hits a guard records *why* on the exchange and on the wire, so it reads as a decision
  rather than as the conversation trailing off. A chain also fails closed: without a
  resolver, no reply is ever treated as a mention.
  Each exchange is announced on its own `room_reply` frame carrying its author, the
  participant who addressed them, and their reply — so a console can render a chain as
  the several messages it actually was. The terminal frame still carries the last reply,
  so a consumer that reads only that gets a sensible answer rather than a transcript.
  A hop is attributed to **the agent that made it**, not to the operator: the room
  records `from="proto"` on proto's mention, and every later participant's catch-up reads
  it back that way.
