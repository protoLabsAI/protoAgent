- **Scheduling now uses croniter 7.** croniter 7.0 changes the meaning of a few rare cron forms. A field that starts at its maximum and is followed by a step, such as `59/15`, `23/6` or `6/1`, now matches only that maximum value, where before it matched across the whole field. croniter 7.0 also rejects some malformed expressions it used to accept, such as non-ASCII digits or an out-of-range `#`. Ordinary schedules (`*/15 * * * *`, `0 9 * * 1`, `15 7,12 * * *`) are unchanged. Every cron schedule stored on the fleet was checked, and each gives the same next run times under 6.2.4 and 7.0.0. If you wrote a schedule like `59/15` and meant "every 15 minutes", change it to `*/15`.
